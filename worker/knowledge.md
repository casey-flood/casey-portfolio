# About Casey Flood (public facts only)

Casey Flood is a product leader and design engineer. She designs in code: she prototypes in production codebases with Storybook, Cursor, and Claude, so what gets tested is what ships. She leads product design across healthcare and dental manufacturing.

## Now: Dandy (Mar 2025 – present), Senior Product Designer
- Dandy makes dental restorations. Casey is the sole product designer on the Design Editor ("Dandy Design Center"), the 3D tool where lab technicians and designers prep scans, design restorations, and check quality across fixed, removable, and implant workflows. Think "Figma for teeth".
- Before that she designed the first dentist-facing ordering and chairside flows for posterior crowns and bridges.
- Chairside (the dentist product) already had a design system called Floss UI. Dandy Design Center had none, built piecemeal by engineers. Casey, the only senior product designer on that side, built a design system from scratch in Storybook with Cursor, on production code. She reused Floss UI components where possible and created many net-new ones. Engineers, PMs, and designers are the system's users: it has code snippets, handoff docs, taxonomy and IA rules, status tags, and documented component states.
- Result: iteration dropped from about 30–45 minutes in Figma to about 5–10 minutes. Page: /work/dandy-design-system
- It was not smooth at first. Getting Storybook, the build pipeline, and 3D scan rendering working took real time, and engineers on the team helped her get it right.
- What it unlocked: building fully clickable prototypes from real components in Storybook (/work/dandy-prototype), and teaching it to others through onboarding, design jams, and the Product Design Hub (/work/dandy-product-design-hub).
- She traveled to Cairo to run in-person usability testing with the people who use the tools every day.
- She uses AI tools (Cursor, Claude, MCPs) as part of how she designs: iterating on real components and testing ideas in code before they become specs.

## Earlier
- Paper Airplane (2017, Boston): first design job at an early-stage dating app. She did "anything and everything": brand, guerrilla marketing, research, UI, and working with developers on APIs. 41 survey responses, 4 interviews, 6 card sorts led to a three-feature MVP and 13 screens. Case study: /work/paper-airplane
- Pratt Institute (New York): formal training in research methods; in-person testing; capstone My Trips, an itinerary creator for spontaneous travelers (94% less time to share a trip in testing). Case study: /work/mytrips
- James Madison University: studied interactive media and design alongside computer information systems, which is why she still prototypes in code.
- Freelance (caseyflood.design): healthcare, finance, travel, construction, and digital media projects alongside full-time roles.
- New York and the Cooper Hewitt museum are where she became obsessed with the psychology and empathy behind design.

## How she works
- Design in the real material: prototype in the production codebase.
- Alignment is a deliverable: she aligns PM, clinical, engineering, and ops as carefully as she designs the screen.
- Curiosity before opinions: shadow people doing the work before drawing anything.
- Measure what matters: every project starts with how we'll know it worked, and ends by checking.
