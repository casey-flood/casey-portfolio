## Problem

Friends ask for recommendations the moment you land, but the trip itself was never written down. The travelers I spoke with — mostly 18 to 30, booking as they went — were spending close to two hours reconstructing a weekend from camera rolls, maps, and half-remembered reservations. About three quarters of the details were gone within a week. The tools they already had made it worse: photos in one app, pins in another, notes nowhere.

The product had to capture a trip without asking someone to become a planner. The point of these trips was that they stayed unplanned.

## Process

I led the work with an iOS team over three months, from research through the interface.

Eight in-depth interviews and a card sort with twelve people showed the same break. People wanted to share a trip, then stalled when sharing meant assembling one. Competitor reviews made the gap clearer. Journaling apps wanted a diary. Itinerary apps wanted a plan made in advance. Neither fit a trip that was still happening.

> **Key decision**
>
> Don't ask people to review the whole trip at once. In testing, that was where they dropped off, so review became a short daily prompt with one-tap sharing at the end.

The concept that held up was a privacy-first timeline built from the camera roll and location data, with a manual override when the automation guessed wrong. I prototyped the flow, tested it, and watched people drop off when the app asked them to review everything at once. Daily review prompts and one-tap sharing came out of those sessions. Research across the project included about thirty participants.

## Impact

In testing, sharing a trip dropped from about ninety minutes to five — a 94% reduction, roughly 85 minutes back per trip. Automated timeline generation was the feature people actually used, and 93% of testers adopted the flow. Privacy controls mattered as much as speed. People shared more when they could see what would leave their phone, and correct it.

Figures are from user testing. The prototype walks through that timeline, from import to a link you can send.
