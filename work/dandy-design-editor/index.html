Design Editor · Sole product designer Still shipping, since 2025

Reimagining how dental designers design restorations.

Dandy’s Design Editor is 3D CAD software that dental designers use to design crowns and bridges from a patient’s scan. One editor for the whole restoration, replacing a chain of legacy and third-party tools.

The short version

Turning a slow, siloed crown design process into one intelligent editor.

The problem
Dandy’s original Design Editor was built fast, by devs, to get out the door. Designing a crown took 3+ handoffs between 3 personas (prepper, designer, auditor), and more time went to moving the file between tools than to designing the tooth. Dental designers rated it 6.9 out of 10 (3Shape: 9.1), and fewer than half used it.

02 · What that chain cost. Summer 2025, before the redesign

3+ programs, and a different person at each one.

Prep in the browser. Design in 3Shape, exocad, or the Dandy Design Editor. Audit in another portal, with scores in a spreadsheet. The case was handed off every time, and the file moved more than the tooth did: download, upload, hunt for the right tool, upload back. That transfer is the waste.Prep, design and audit each happened in a different tool, so the file moved more than the tooth did.

14.5 min average crown design time, including waits between steps (Q1 2025)

Inside the Dandy Design Editor, the tools were buried.

Nested menus meant dental designers hunted for the next click instead of designing.Nested menus meant dental designers hunted for the next click instead of designing. Their other top complaints: too many clicks, and hotkeys that didn’t match 3Shape.

“I wish it could be, like, going stages or maybe have it on the top… group related tools together.”

Dental designer, DDE designer interviews

Satisfaction was low next to the competition.

Before the redesign, dental designers scored the Dandy Design Editor 6.9 out of 10, against 9.1 for 3Shape. The usual complaints: nested menus and too many clicks, hotkeys that didn’t match 3Shape, and tools they had to leave for, like the cut plane.

Fewer than half of dental designers used the Dandy Design Editor.

About 46% of dental designers did their crown design in it, 43% in Q4 2024 and 49% in Q1 2025. The goal was 100%.

04 · The hypotheses

I framed three hypotheses to bring design time down and quality up.

Each is testable, and I proposed how to measure it.

  1. 1ML-generated crowns
  2. 2Prep in the editor, then automated
  3. 3Navigation and IA in parallel

Hypothesis 1If ML generates a bio-mimetic starting crown, time drops and quality rises.

I designed how designers start and regenerate a crown: from the ML crown, the tooth library, or a mimic tooth.

Measured by

  • Design time
  • Refab rate
  • Review rejections

ML-touched vs. untouched crowns

Hypothesis 2If prep lives in the editor and runs itself, handoffs and waits drop.

I brought prep into the editor so Dandy could automate it. Dental designers audit it instead, saving time in one ecosystem.

Measured by

  • Scan-to-submit time
  • Programs and people per case

Hypothesis 3If navigation follows how designers think, cognitive load drops.

I rebuilt navigation around the stages designers work through: Prep, Front, Crown, Design, Submit.

Measured by

  • Satisfaction (6.9 vs. 3Shape’s 9.1)
  • Time hunting for tools
  • Dental designer adoption (~46% at baseline)

08 · Reflections

What was hard.

Shipping ML crowns, prep and the new navigation at the same time makes it hard to attribute a result to any one change. Dental designers kept the generated crown about 35% of the time and redid it about 45%, so trust in automation has to be earned. And guidance has to stay out of the way: designers worried about intrusive blockers, so hard stops are for Rx and doctor-note violations, and everything else is advisory.Shipping ML crowns, prep and navigation together makes it hard to credit any one change. Trust in automation has to be earned: dental designers kept the generated crown about 35% of the time and redid it about 45%. Hard stops are only for Rx and doctor-note violations; everything else stays advisory.

What I’m still tracking.

Quality. The refab rate has held at 6.3–6.5% every quarter while crown volume grew, so speed hasn’t cost quality, but I can’t yet show it improved. Design review rejection records have gaps, so I’m not relying on them yet. I’m also re-running the dental designer survey and measuring time per step as automated prep and the next releases roll out. Several changes shipped at once, so isolating what each one did is the hard part.Quality: the refab rate held at 6.3–6.5% every quarter as volume grew, so speed hasn’t cost quality, but I can’t yet show it improved. I’m re-running the dental designer survey and timing each step as releases roll out. Several changes shipped at once, so isolating each is the hard part.

What’s next.

I’ll keep releasing the ideal version in chunks: more of the guided flow and navigation, then the same architecture for bridges, implants and other case types.I’ll keep releasing the ideal version in chunks, then bring the same architecture to bridges, implants and other case types.

09 · Forward thinking, part of the reflections

What if designers became auditors?

As more crowns are fully automated, the designer’s job may shift from building every restoration to auditing what the model produced. This phone concept imagines that world: a lightweight companion for the queue, the case brief, and quick checks on automated work. It’s a thought experiment about scaling this system and changing the nature of design, not a roadmap item. There is no mobile Design Editor today. Sample data only.
