The short version

Turning a slow, siloed crown design process into one intelligent editor.

The problem
Dandy’s original Design Editor was built fast, by devs, to get out the door. Designing a crown took 3+ handoffs between 3 personas (prepper, designer, auditor), and more time went to moving the file between tools than to designing the tooth. Designers rated it 6.9 out of 10 (3Shape: 9.1), and fewer than half used it.Built fast by devs, the original editor took 3+ handoffs between 3 personas per crown, and more time went to moving files than designing the tooth. Designers rated it 6.9/10 (3Shape: 9.1), and fewer than half used it.
What I owned
The goals: design time down without lowering quality, and designers choosing our own editor over 3Shape. As the sole product designer, I set the direction, ran the research, built the design system, and prototyped in code to test real cases with designers. 02 · What that chain cost. Summer 2025, before the redesign

3+ programs, and a different person at each one.

Prep in the browser. Design in 3Shape, exocad, or the Dandy Design Editor. Audit in another portal, with scores in a spreadsheet. The case was handed off every time, and the file moved more than the tooth did: download, upload, hunt for the right tool, upload back. That transfer is the waste.Prep in the browser, design in 3Shape, exocad or our editor, audit in another portal with scores in a spreadsheet. Inside the Dandy Design Editor, the tools were buried.

Nested menus meant designers hunted for the next click instead of designing.

“I wish it could be, like, going stages or maybe have it on the top… group related tools together.”

Satisfaction was low next to the competition.

I found that navigation was a problem on its own.

Tools were grouped by category, not by how designers work, so they hunted through nested menus for the next step. ML and automated prep wouldn’t fix that, so navigation needed its own redesign. 04 · The hypotheses

I framed three hypotheses to bring design time down and quality up.

Each is testable, and I proposed how to measure it.

  1. 1ML-generated crowns
  2. 2Prep in the editor, then automated
  3. 3Navigation and IA in parallel

Hypothesis 1If ML generates a bio-mimetic starting crown, time drops and quality rises.

I designed how designers start and regenerate a crown: from the ML crown, the tooth library, or a mimic tooth.

Measured by

  • Design time
  • Refab rate
  • Review rejections

ML-touched vs. untouched crowns

Hypothesis 2If prep lives in the editor and runs itself, handoffs and waits drop.

I brought prep into the editor so Dandy could automate it. Designers audit it instead, saving time in one ecosystem.

Measured by

  • Scan-to-submit time
  • Programs and people per case

Hypothesis 3If navigation follows how designers think, cognitive load drops.

I rebuilt navigation around the stages designers work through: Prep, Front, Crown, Design, Submit.

Measured by

  • Satisfaction (6.9 vs. 3Shape’s 9.1)
  • Time hunting for tools
  • Designer adoption (~46% at baseline)

08 · Reflections

What was hard.

Shipping ML crowns, prep and the new navigation at the same time makes it hard to attribute a result to any one change. Designers kept the generated crown about 35% of the time and redid it about 45%, so trust in automation has to be earned. And guidance has to stay out of the way: designers worried about intrusive blockers, so hard stops are for Rx and doctor-note violations, and everything else is advisory.Shipping ML crowns, prep and navigation together makes it hard to credit any one change. Trust in automation has to be earned: designers kept the generated crown about 35% of the time and redid it about 45%. Hard stops are only for Rx and doctor-note violations; everything else stays advisory.

What I’m still tracking.

Quality. The refab rate has held at 6.3–6.5% every quarter while crown volume grew, so speed hasn’t cost quality, but I can’t yet show it improved. Design review rejection records have gaps, so I’m not relying on them yet. I’m also re-running the designer survey and measuring time per step as automated prep and the next releases roll out. Several changes shipped at once, so isolating what each one did is the hard part.Quality: the refab rate held at 6.3–6.5% every quarter as volume grew, so speed hasn’t cost quality, but I can’t yet show it improved. I’m re-running the designer survey and timing each step as releases roll out. Several changes shipped at once, so isolating each is the hard part.

What’s next.

I’ll keep releasing the ideal version in chunks: more of the guided flow and navigation, then the same architecture for bridges, implants and other case types.I’ll keep releasing the ideal version in chunks, then bring the same architecture to bridges, implants and other case types.

09 · Forward thinking, part of the reflections

What if designers became auditors?

As more crowns are fully automated, the designer’s job may shift from building every restoration to auditing what the model produced. This phone concept imagines that world: a lightweight companion for the queue, the case brief, and quick checks on automated work. It’s a thought experiment about scaling this system and changing the nature of design, not a roadmap item. There is no mobile Design Editor today. Sample data only.
