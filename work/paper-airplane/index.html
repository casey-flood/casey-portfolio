01 · The bet

How do we make meeting in person less intimidating?

Swiping keeps people behind screens. Paper Airplane flips it: see how many compatible people are at a venue near you, then go meet them there.

04 · Working with developers

Even in 2017, I designed alongside the APIs.

Screen by screen, I worked with the developers to understand what each view needed from the API, and what depended on what. The biggest rule: where you are decides what you see.

  1. Open the app
  2. Inside a venue?
  3. Yes → Current venue
  4. No → Heat map

Facebook login

A first-time user signs in with Facebook. We mapped exactly which permissions the app needed.

permissions:
  public_profile
  email
  user_birthday

Outside a venue

If you are logged in but not inside a geo-fenced venue, you land on the heat map.

GET /venues
// all venues geo-fenced by the app

Inside a venue

Step inside a geo-fenced venue and the app loads that venue and the people checked in.

GET /venue/{venueGUID}
// unique ID per registered venue

Discovery preferences

Changes apply instantly. Hiding your profile had to be secured, so no one could hide or impersonate someone else.

PUT /user/{fbId}
PUT /user/{fbId}/hide
// secured: only you can hide you

Messaging

Only mutually accepted users can chat, and messages are stored on our servers, not a third party.

POST /conversation
GET /conversation/{from}/{to}

05 · Testing

Four tests. Three fixes.

I built a clickable Figma prototype and tested it on UserTesting.com, focusing on the venue map and check-in.

The map was overwhelming

What we found

4 of 4 testers said Google Maps showed too many icons. They struggled to spot our logo and wanted a smaller area and a list view.

What I changed

Fewer pins, a calmer map, and a Map and List toggle.

Drag to compare an earlier iteration with the final design.

IterationFinal

The venue view buried what mattered

What we found

The details were useful, but the layout felt long and confusing, and the match count was easy to miss.

What I changed

Matches, shared interests, and distance now lead the screen, with one clear Check in button.

Drag to compare an earlier iteration with the final design.

IterationFinal

The icons were confusing

What we found

Our logo looked like the send-message icon, directions had no icon, and Check in was not intuitive.

What I changed

Each action got its own clear, labeled control.

What I took from it

My first project wearing almost every hat.

Researcher

Uncovering insights that guided intuitive interfaces.

UI designer

Turning research into visuals, learning from Figma kits on the go.

Product manager

Balancing user needs with business goals and timelines.

Solutions architect

Designing within what the technology, like APIs, could actually do.

What I'd measure next: A/B tests, heatmaps, and funnel analysis, to pair our qualitative findings with numbers. The team couldn't resource more, so my time ended after the first round of visual design.
